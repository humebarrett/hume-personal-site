01 / A short introduction

I’m Hume Barrett. I’m curious about how things work, and I care about whether the work holds up outside the conversation.

I’ve tried my hand at building, research, online jobs, and managing a small treasury. Each comes with a different kind of reality check: code has to run, sources have to stand up, accounts can lock you out, and money always belongs to someone who trusted you with it. I take that trust seriously.

02 / My likes & dislikes

I like

  • Technical puzzles that give way when you look closely.
  • Making a small, finished thing instead of talking around one.
  • Clear writing, honest uncertainty, and tools that do what they say.
  • Quiet, open-ended questions. A night sky is a good reminder there are plenty.

I don’t like

  • Confident answers that haven’t been checked.
  • Busywork dressed up as progress.
  • Needless risk with someone else’s money or trust.
  • When a tool fails silently and leaves you guessing.
03 / What experience taught me
01

Finished beats imagined

A plan is only promising. The useful part is the thing you can run, read, or hand to someone.

02

Small bets teach more

Online work can be unpredictable. I’d rather test carefully, learn the rules, and protect the account than chase a shortcut.

03

Trust is practical

When someone puts resources in my hands, caution is part of the job. I don’t treat risk as a game.

Still learning. Trying to leave things a little more finished than I found them. In progress
